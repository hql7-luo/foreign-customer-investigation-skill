# Evidence and Non-fabrication Rules

## Evidence states

### Confirmed

Use when any is true:

- An official registry or government source supports the fact.
- A company website and a registry, map, or other reliable source cross-verify it.
- Two or more independent reliable sources agree.

### Partially confirmed

Use when support consists only of:

- One company-owned website
- A registry aggregator
- A business card, exhibition directory, or customer statement
- Old information whose current validity cannot be confirmed

### Inferred

Use for a reasoned judgment based on:

- Consumable-product characteristics
- Printing process
- Equipment maintenance cycle
- Industry seasonality
- Exhibition timing
- Production cycle
- Business model

State the basis in the same sentence. Never present the inference as fact.

### Conflicting information

Use when sources disagree on legal/company name, address, phone, legal representative, contact, website owner, or registered entity. Preserve all material variants and list the conflict as a risk or uncertainty. Do not select one as certain without adequate evidence.

### Not found

Use when reliable public evidence could not be located. This status does not mean the thing does not exist.

## Prohibited fabrication

Never invent:

- Revenue
- Employee count
- Capacity
- Customer list
- Supplier
- Cooperation brand
- Import record
- Customs data
- Equipment model
- Purchase quantity
- Purchase cycle

Use the report-language equivalent of:

- `未找到可靠公开信息 / No reliable public information was found`
- `未找到公开可靠记录 / No reliable public record was found`
- `无法确认 / Unable to confirm`

Do not use unsupported promotional language such as “large scale,” “strong company,” or “industry leader.”

## Absence and conflict rules

Write “No reliable public record was found,” not:

- Has no customers
- Has no suppliers
- Has never imported
- Has no purchase requirement
- Has no production equipment

When records conflict:

1. Preserve each differing value.
2. Cite the source and information date/year.
3. Mark it `conflict`.
4. Explain the commercial consequence.
5. Ask the customer for a primary document or clarification when appropriate.

## Website limitations

A company website can confirm only that the company publicly presents the stated business. It does not independently establish:

- Actual capacity
- Actual purchases
- Real customer lists
- Real cooperation brands
- Current scale
- That every product remains available

Treat a single company website as partially confirmed unless cross-verified.

## Timeliness

Always record the investigation date. Prefer the latest source for registration status, contact details, sanctions, recruiting, and operating status.

Label information older than two years with its year, for example:

- “The company’s 2023 news page stated …”
- “As of 2024 …”
- “A 2022 exhibition directory listed …”

Do not restate historical information as current fact. When a source has no publication date, record the access date and say the content date cannot be confirmed.
